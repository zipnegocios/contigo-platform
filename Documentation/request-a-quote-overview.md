# Request a Quote — What It Is and Why It Matters

Audience: business stakeholders. No code, no technical jargon — just what
happens and why it's built this way.

## The problem this solves

Before this workflow, every quote request would need someone to manually check
an inbox, reply to the customer, and keep track of where each conversation
stood. That doesn't scale, and it's easy for a request to fall through the
cracks. This workflow automates the parts that don't need a human, and gives
the team a shared, organized view of every request that does.

## The journey, end to end

**1. A visitor asks for a quote.**
Someone on the website fills out the "Request a Quote" form — their name,
contact details, the service they're interested in, a description of the
project, and optionally a few photos. They hit submit.

**2. Two things happen immediately, automatically.**
- The visitor gets a confirmation email, with a personal link where they can
  track the status of their request at any time — no account or login needed.
- The team gets notified internally that a new request has come in, with all
  the details.

At the same time, behind the scenes, the request is already sitting in the
team's pipeline as a new lead — nobody has to manually add it.

**3. The team works the lead through a pipeline.**
Every lead moves through stages (for example: New → Contacted → In Progress →
Won/Lost — the exact stage names are configurable). Staff can:
- Schedule and track calls, site visits, and meetings.
- Keep a running checklist of tasks that need to happen for this project.
- Take internal notes that only the team sees.
- Message back and forth with the client directly through the same tracking
  link the client received — no separate email thread to lose track of.
- Attach documents — reference photos the client sent, or quotes/contracts the
  team sends back.

**4. The client stays in the loop automatically.**
Whenever the team moves the lead to a new stage, schedules a visit, or sends a
message, the client gets an email letting them know — and can always check
their private tracking page for the current status, upcoming appointments, and
message history. They never have to ask "what's going on with my quote?"

**5. Leads that don't go anywhere get cleaned up, safely.**
If a lead is no longer relevant, staff can archive it (keep it, out of the way)
or move it to trash (a safety net before deleting). Permanently deleting a lead
is a separate, deliberately harder action — it requires typing the client's
name to confirm, because it erases everything about that lead for good,
including the original request and every attachment. That's by design: it's
hard to do by accident, but still possible when genuinely needed (e.g. spam,
duplicate submissions, a client's data-removal request).

## Why it's designed this way

- **Nothing sits unnoticed.** The moment someone submits the form, both the
  client and the team are told immediately, and the request is already
  trackable in the pipeline — there's no manual step where it could be missed.
- **The client never has to chase an update.** Every meaningful change
  triggers a notification and is reflected on their tracking page in real time.
- **The team has one place to work from.** Calls, tasks, notes, documents, and
  the conversation with the client all live on the same lead, instead of being
  scattered across email, phone notes, and spreadsheets.
- **Deleting is intentionally a two-step, confirmed action** (trash, then a
  confirmed permanent delete) so accidental data loss is very unlikely, while
  still allowing real cleanup when it's genuinely wanted.
